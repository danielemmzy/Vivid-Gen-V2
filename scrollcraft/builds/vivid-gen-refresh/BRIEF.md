# Vivid Gen design brief

**Self-authored under explicit creative delegation.** The user asked for original design decisions and supplied the direction below. No user interview or invented quotations.

## Eight-topic brief

1. **Vibe and references** — The user said, “Keep the current identity (Recommended).” Keep Vivid’s dark cinematic red identity. They asked for a polished, professional look with a consistent visual style across landing, sign-in, and sign-up.
2. **Visitor journey** — Self-authored: recognize a creator’s ambition; see how one idea becomes a directed plan; explore a small set of video workflows and bundles; choose a route into creation.
3. **Energy curve** — Self-authored: confident and spacious at the opening; curious as the production plan changes; focused while workflows and bundles become clear; calm and decisive at sign-up.
4. **Feeling and remembered moment** — Self-authored peak: “I switched the kind of video I wanted and watched the same brief reshape into a different shot plan.” Peak lives in the hero’s interactive brief-to-plan preview.
5. **Distinctive thing the site does** — Self-authored: one compact creative brief can reconfigure the on-page storyboard across campaign, product, and podcast examples.
6. **Aesthetic range** — Dark cinematic with bright, natural human photography, open spacing, warm red accents, translucent floating production panels, and light motion. Avoid a generic dashboard-template feel.
7. **One world or distinct scenes** — Self-authored: distinct but visually related scenes. Use one photographic color grade and repeat the same rounded glass/paperwork language across landing and authentication.
8. **Existing assets** — Existing Vivid dark/red identity and application workflows. The user asked for photos chosen for aspiring video creators, plus LottieFiles animation. No user-owned photo or brand-kit files were identified for this build.

## Step 1: brief and journey

**What this is and who it is for:** Vivid Gen is an AI video production workspace for creators, small teams, and studios who want one idea to become a finished production.

**What the visitor should believe:** A short brief can become a plan they can review and direct before generation.

**Next action:** Start creating.

**Art direction:** Low-key cinematic human photography, with natural skin tones and warm practical light. Keep the scene authentic and the overlays minimal. Reuse the selected photography across the landing and auth screens for continuity.

### Journey

1. **Recognition:** a real creator and collaborator are already shaping a film together.
2. **Curiosity:** the visitor changes the sample brief and sees the shot plan adapt.
3. **Clarity:** the Director’s review and approval flow makes the process concrete.
4. **Range:** a short set of workflow bundles maps to real production intent.
5. **Commitment:** a clean, cohesive sign-in or sign-up entry.

### Feeling curve

- Recognition — documentary creator photography presents a plausible version of the visitor’s own studio.
- Curiosity — changing a sample workflow reconfigures the storyboard in the hero.
- Confidence — a compact three-step production sequence shows plan, approval, and delivery.
- Resolve — a featured workflow bundle leads into the existing sign-up route.

### Peak

“I switched the kind of video I wanted and watched the same brief reshape into a different shot plan.” It lives in the hero.

### Tell-someone sentence

It’s the site where you switch the kind of video you want and watch one brief become a different shot plan.

### Authored silence

Hold a clean breathing beat between the workflow proof and the bundle selection. Keep the final action visually quiet and resolved.
